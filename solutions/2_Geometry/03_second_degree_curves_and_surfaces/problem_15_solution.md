# Problem 15: Recognition and Sketch Without a Given Name

## 1. The Original Problem

Consider the two equations:

$$
4x^2 + 9y^2 - 8x + 36y + 4 = 0,
$$

$$
4x^2 + y^2 = z^2.
$$

For the first equation, reduce it to canonical form, recognize the curve, give its most important features, and make a sketch.

For the second equation, do not begin by using a ready-made name for the surface. First describe the cross-sections $z = c$ and the cross-sections by the planes $x = 0$ and $y = 0$, paying attention to the different scales along the axes. Only then identify the surface and make a sketch.

---

## 2. What Are We Trying to Find?

We need to:
1. **Analyze Equation 1 ($4x^2 + 9y^2 - 8x + 36y + 4 = 0$):**
   - Complete squares in $x$ and $y$ to convert the general second-degree equation to canonical form $\frac{(x - x_0)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y - y_0)^2}{b^2} = 1$.
   - Identify the 2D curve (**ellipse**).
   - Determine its primary geometric features: center $(x_0, y_0)$, semimajor axis $a$, semiminor axis $b$, vertices, co-vertices, linear eccentricity $c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$, foci $F_1, F_2$, and eccentricity $e$.
   - Provide a qualitative sketch and diagram.
2. **Analyze Equation 2 ($4x^2 + y^2 = z^2$):**
   - Follow a bottom-up geometric approach (traces first, naming last).
   - Analyze horizontal slices $z = c$, noting that the coefficients $4$ and $1$ produce ellipses stretched along the $y$-axis by a factor of 2 compared to $x$.
   - Analyze vertical slices $x = 0$ ($yz$-plane) and $y = 0$ ($xz$-plane), observing their slopes.
   - Synthesize these observations to formally identify the surface (**elliptic cone**).
   - Provide a qualitative 3D sketch highlighting the elliptical shape of its cross-sections.

---

## 3. Before Calculating: What Idea Do We Need?

- **Completing the Square for 2D Conics:**
  A quadratic form $A x^2 + C y^2 + D x + E y + F = 0$ without a mixed term $xy$ represents a conic aligned with the coordinate axes.
  Grouping terms:

$$
A\left(x^2 + \frac{D}{A}x\right) + C\left(y^2 + \frac{E}{C}y\right) = -F.
$$

  Completing squares reveals the translation vector $(x_0, y_0)$. If both leading coefficients $A, C > 0$ and the resulting right-hand side is positive, the curve is an **ellipse**.
- **Trace-First 3D Surface Analysis:**
  Instead of memorizing names, examine how the object looks from three orthogonal perspectives:
  - Horizontal slice $z = c$: $4x^2 + y^2 = c^2 \iff \frac{x^2}{(|c|/2)^2} + \frac{y^2}{|c|^2} = 1$. The axes have lengths $|c|/2$ (along $x$) and $|c|$ (along $y$). Because the semiaxes are in ratio $1 : 2$, each slice is an ellipse stretched twice as far along $y$ as along $x$.
  - Vertical slices $x = 0$ and $y = 0$: produce straight lines passing through the origin.
  - A surface generated by straight lines through the origin whose horizontal cross-sections are ellipses is an **elliptic cone**.

---

## 4. Step-by-Step Solution

### Part 1: Equation 1 ($4x^2 + 9y^2 - 8x + 36y + 4 = 0$)

#### Step 1: Group Terms by Variable
Group terms containing $x$ and terms containing $y$, moving the constant to the right-hand side:

$$
(4x^2 - 8x) + (9y^2 + 36y) = -4
$$

Factor out the leading coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$:

$$
4(x^2 - 2x) + 9(y^2 + 4y) = -4
$$

#### Step 2: Complete the Squares Inside Parentheses
- For $x$: $x^2 - 2x = (x - 1)^2 - 1^2 = (x - 1)^2 - 1$
- For $y$: $y^2 + 4y = (y + 2)^2 - 2^2 = (y + 2)^2 - 4$

Substitute these into the equation:

$$
4\left[(x - 1)^2 - 1\right] + 9\left[(y + 2)^2 - 4\right] = -4
$$

Distribute the coefficients:

$$
4(x - 1)^2 - 4 + 9(y + 2)^2 - 36 = -4
$$

Combine constant terms on the right-hand side:

$$
4(x - 1)^2 + 9(y + 2)^2 = -4 + 4 + 36 \implies 4(x - 1)^2 + 9(y + 2)^2 = 36
$$

#### Step 3: Divide by 36 to Reach Canonical Form
Divide both sides by $36$:

$$
\frac{4(x - 1)^2}{36} + \frac{9(y + 2)^2}{36} = \frac{36}{36}
$$

$$
\frac{(x - 1)^2}{9} + \frac{(y + 2)^2}{4} = 1
$$

Writing the denominators as explicit squares:

$$
\frac{(x - 1)^2}{3^2} + \frac{(y + 2)^2}{2^2} = 1
$$

#### Step 4: Extract All Geometric Features
This is the canonical equation of a translated **ellipse** with horizontal major axis ($a > b$):

1. **Center:**

$$
S(x_0, y_0) = (1, -2)
$$

2. **Semiaxes:**
   - Horizontal semimajor axis: $a = 3$ (along line $y = -2$)
   - Vertical semiminor axis: $b = 2$ (along line $x = 1$)
3. **Vertices (Endpoints of the Major Axis):**

$$
A_1(x_0 - a, y_0) = (1 - 3, -2) = (-2, -2)
$$

$$
A_2(x_0 + a, y_0) = (1 + 3, -2) = (4, -2)
$$

4. **Co-vertices (Endpoints of the Minor Axis):**

$$
B_1(x_0, y_0 - b) = (1, -2 - 2) = (1, -4)
$$

$$
B_2(x_0, y_0 + b) = (1, -2 + 2) = (1, 0)
$$

5. **Linear Eccentricity ($c_{\text{focal}}$):**

$$
c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = \sqrt{9 - 4} = \sqrt{5} \approx 2.236
$$

6. **Foci:**
   Located at $(x_0 \pm c, y_0)$ along the horizontal major axis:

$$
F_1(1 - \sqrt{5}, -2) \approx (-1.236, -2)
$$

$$
F_2(1 + \sqrt{5}, -2) \approx (3.236, -2)
$$

7. **Numerical Eccentricity:**

$$
e = \frac{c}{a} = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{3} \approx 0.745
$$

#### Step 5: Qualitative 2D Sketch
```
              y
              ^
              |         B2(1, 0)
         -----+------------*------------> x
              |         |  |
              |         |  |
      A1(-2,-2) *---F1----S(1,-2)----F2---* A2(4,-2)   <- major axis y = -2
              |         |  |
              |         |  |
              |         B1(1, -4)
              |
```
- Center at $(1, -2)$.
- Stretches horizontally from $x = -2$ to $x = 4$ (width $2a = 6$).
- Stretches vertically from $y = -4$ to $y = 0$ (height $2b = 4$).
- Tangent to the $x$-axis at the single point $B_2(1, 0)$ because $y = 0 \implies \frac{(x - 1)^2}{9} + \frac{4}{4} = 1 \implies (x - 1)^2 = 0 \implies x = 1$.

---

### Part 2: Equation 2 ($4x^2 + y^2 = z^2$)

We follow the strict instruction to examine cross-sections first before naming the surface.

#### 1. Horizontal Cross-Sections $z = c$:
Intersect the surface with the horizontal plane $z = c$:

$$
4x^2 + y^2 = c^2
$$

- **For $c = 0$ ($xy$-plane):**

$$
4x^2 + y^2 = 0 \implies x = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad y = 0
$$

  The slice at $z = 0$ is the single point $(0, 0, 0)$.
- **For $c \ne 0$:**
  Divide both sides by $c^2$:

$$
\frac{4x^2}{c^2} + \frac{y^2}{c^2} = 1 \implies \frac{x^2}{(|c|/2)^2} + \frac{y^2}{|c|^2} = 1
$$

  In the horizontal plane $z = c$, this represents an **ellipse** centered at $(0, 0, c)$ with:
  - Semiaxis along $x$: $a_x(c) = \frac{|c|}{2}$
  - Semiaxis along $y$: $b_y(c) = |c|$
  - **Scale Comparison:** The semiaxis in the $y$-direction is exactly twice as long as the semiaxis in the $x$-direction ($b_y = 2 a_x$). Every horizontal cross-section is an ellipse elongated along the $y$-axis by a factor of 2.
  - As $|c|$ increases, the elliptical cross-sections expand linearly in direct proportion to $|c|$.

#### 2. Vertical Cross-Section $x = 0$ ($yz$-plane):
Substitute $x = 0$ into $4x^2 + y^2 = z^2$:

$$
4(0)^2 + y^2 = z^2 \implies y^2 = z^2 \implies y^2 - z^2 = 0 \implies (y - z)(y + z) = 0
$$

This yields two straight lines:

$$
z = y \quad \text{and} \quad z = -y
$$

- **Description:** A **pair of straight lines** intersecting at $(0, 0, 0)$ with slope $\pm 1$ ($45^\circ$ angle with the horizontal $y$-axis).

#### 3. Vertical Cross-Section $y = 0$ ($xz$-plane):
Substitute $y = 0$ into $4x^2 + y^2 = z^2$:

$$
4x^2 + 0^2 = z^2 \implies z^2 = 4x^2 \implies z^2 - (2x)^2 = 0 \implies (z - 2x)(z + 2x) = 0
$$

This yields two straight lines:

$$
z = 2x \quad \text{and} \quad z = -2x
$$

- **Description:** A **pair of straight lines** intersecting at $(0, 0, 0)$ with steeper slope $\pm 2$. Because the slope is steeper in the $xz$-plane than in the $yz$-plane, the surface rises faster along $x$ than along $y$, which is why the horizontal elliptical slices are narrower along $x$ ($a_x = |c|/2$) and wider along $y$ ($b_y = |c|$).

#### 4. Identification of the Surface:
Having determined that:
1. Horizontal slices are ellipses centered on the $z$-axis with semiaxes in constant ratio $1 : 2$,
2. Vertical slices through the origin are straight lines,
3. The surface meets tip-to-tip at the origin $(0, 0, 0)$,

we conclusively identify the surface as an **Elliptic Cone** (with vertical axis $Oz$ and vertex at $(0, 0, 0)$).

#### 5. Qualitative 3D Sketch of the Elliptic Cone
```
               z
               ^
            . --- .          <- elliptical slice at z = c > 0
          /    |    \           semiaxes: x-radius = c/2, y-radius = c
         |     |     |          (elongated along y-axis)
          \    |    /
           \   |   /
            \  |  /
               *             <- apex / vertex at origin (0, 0, 0)
            /  |  \
           /   |   \
          /    |    \
         |     |     |       <- elliptical slice at z = -c < 0
          \    |    /
            ' --- '
               |
      ---------+---------> y
             /
            v x
```

## 5. Final Answer & Conclusion

### Summary for Equation 1:
- **Canonical Form:**

$$
\frac{(x - 1)^2}{9} + \frac{(y + 2)^2}{4} = 1 \quad \left(\text{or } \frac{(x - 1)^2}{3^2} + \frac{(y + 2)^2}{2^2} = 1\right)
$$

- **Curve Type:** **Ellipse**
- **Center:** $S(1, -2)$
- **Semiaxes:** $a = 3$ (horizontal, semimajor), $b = 2$ (vertical, semiminor)
- **Vertices:** $(-2, -2)$ and $(4, -2)$
- **Co-vertices:** $(1, -4)$ and $(1, 0)$
- **Foci:** $F_1(1 - \sqrt{5}, -2)$ and $F_2(1 + \sqrt{5}, -2)$
- **Linear Eccentricity:** $c = \sqrt{5} \approx 2.236$
- **Numerical Eccentricity:** $e = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{3} \approx 0.745$

### Summary for Equation 2:
- **Trace $z = c \ne 0$:** Ellipses $\frac{x^2}{(|c|/2)^2} + \frac{y^2}{|c|^2} = 1$, elongated along $y$ with semiaxes $a_x = |c|/2$ and $b_y = |c|$.
- **Trace $z = 0$:** Apex point $(0, 0, 0)$.
- **Trace $x = 0$:** Intersecting lines $z = \pm y$ (slope $\pm 1$).
- **Trace $y = 0$:** Intersecting lines $z = \pm 2x$ (slope $\pm 2$).
- **Identified Surface:** **Elliptic Cone** with vertex at the origin and vertical axis along $Oz$.

## 6. Quick Verification

1. **Verify Equation 1 Algebraically:**
   Expand the canonical form:

$$
4(x - 1)^2 + 9(y + 2)^2 = 4(x^2 - 2x + 1) + 9(y^2 + 4y + 4) = 4x^2 - 8x + 4 + 9y^2 + 36y + 36
$$

   Sum of constant terms: $4 + 36 = 40$.
   Subtracting 36 from both sides:

$$
4x^2 + 9y^2 - 8x + 36y + (40 - 36) = 4x^2 + 9y^2 - 8x + 36y + 4 = 0.
$$

   The expanded equation matches the original equation exactly.

2. **Verify Point on Equation 1:**
   Substitute the upper co-vertex $(1, 0)$:

$$
4(1)^2 + 9(0)^2 - 8(1) + 36(0) + 4 = 4 - 8 + 4 = 0 \quad \text{(correct)}.
$$

   Substitute the vertex $(4, -2)$:

$$
4(16) + 9(4) - 8(4) + 36(-2) + 4 = 64 + 36 - 32 - 72 + 4 = 0 \quad \text{(correct)}.
$$

3. **Verify Scale Ratio of Equation 2:**
   For $z = 2$:

$$
4x^2 + y^2 = 4 \implies x^2 + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1.
$$

   The semiaxes are $a_x = 1$ and $b_y = 2$, exactly in ratio $1 : 2$.

## 7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Sign Errors When Moving Constants in Completing the Square:**
   Remember that adding 1 inside $4(x - 1)^2$ adds $4 \times 1 = 4$ to the equation. Adding 4 inside $9(y + 2)^2$ adds $9 \times 4 = 36$. Both must be accounted for on the right-hand side.
2. **Calling $4x^2 + y^2 = z^2$ a Circular Cone:**
   The coefficients of $x^2$ ($4$) and $y^2$ ($1$) are different. A circular cone requires equal coefficients ($x^2 + y^2 = z^2$). Different coefficients make the horizontal cross-sections **ellipses**, making the surface an **elliptic cone**.
3. **Inverting the Semiaxes of the Ellipse:**
   In $4x^2 + y^2 = c^2$, the coefficient 4 on $x^2$ means $x^2 / (c/2)^2$, so the $x$-semiaxis is the **smaller** one ($|c|/2$), while the $y$-semiaxis is the **larger** one ($|c|$).

## 8. How I Would Explain This in Class

> "In this exercise, we master two fundamental skills of geometry: algebra to geometry, and slices to 3D shape.
> For the first equation, it looks messy with linear terms $-8x$ and $+36y$. But completing the square cleans it up completely, packing it into $\frac{(x-1)^2}{9} + \frac{(y+2)^2}{4} = 1$. Instantly, we see it's an ellipse centered at $(1, -2)$, stretching $3$ units horizontally and $2$ units vertically.
> For the second equation, don't jump to conclusions! Slice it first. If you slice horizontally at height $z = c$, you get $4x^2 + y^2 = c^2$. If the coefficients were equal, you'd have circles. But because of that $4$ in front of $x^2$, the ellipse is compressed horizontally—it is twice as tall along $y$ as it is wide along $x$. Vertical slices through the axes give straight lines meeting at the origin. What has straight lines radiating from a point and elliptical cross-sections? An elliptic cone—like a regular cone that has been squashed sideways!"
